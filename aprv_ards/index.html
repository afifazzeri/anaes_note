1. Overview & Rationale

What it is: APRV is a pressure-controlled, time-cycled mode that maintains a constant high airway pressure (Phigh) for most of the respiratory cycle, interrupted by brief pressure releases (Tlow) to a low pressure (Plow). It is essentially CPAP with intermittent, very short expiratory periods.

Core idea: Driving pressure (ΔP between PEEP and inspiratory pressure) is the main injurious force in ARDS. APRV minimises cyclic atelectasis by keeping alveoli open at a high mean airway pressure, while allowing CO₂ clearance during brief releases. Spontaneous breathing is preserved throughout.

Key conceptual point: AutoPEEP is central to the mechanism — gas trapping maintains alveolar recruitment. In conventional ventilation, autoPEEP is an unwanted side effect; in APRV, it is the therapeutic goal.

Historical note: First described by Stock et al. (1987). Still controversial; not first-line in most guidelines, but used as a rescue strategy in severe ARDS.

2. The Four Variables (Tlow, Plow, Phigh, Thigh)
Variable What it does Typical starting value Adjustment logic
Tlow
Time at low pressure		 Determines derecruitment risk; the most important setting for lung protection. 0.3–0.5 s Titrate to 75% of peak expiratory flow (PEFR). Shorter if stiff lungs (0.3 s); longer as compliance improves (0.5 s+).
Plow
Release pressure		 Sets the pressure gradient for expiratory flow and CO₂ clearance. 0 cm H₂O (or 5 cm H₂O) Keep minimal. If hypoxia persists, increase Plow to raise autoPEEP and reduce derecruitment.
Phigh
High pressure		 Maintains alveolar recruitment and oxygenation; safety ceiling. ≈ 28 cm H₂O (≤ 30) Set near the previous plateau pressure. Reduce slowly as the patient improves.
Thigh
Time at high pressure		 Controls CO₂ clearance (minute volume) and is the primary weaning variable. ≈ 9× Tlow (e.g. 4.5 s for Tlow 0.5 s) Reduce Thigh to increase breath rate if CO₂ high. Increase Thigh during weaning.

Default starting settings example: Phigh 28, Plow 0, Thigh 4.5 s, Tlow 0.5 s → I:E ≈ 9:1, rate ≈ 12/min.

4. Advantages Pro
  • Maximal alveolar recruitment: High mean airway pressure keeps alveoli open, minimising cyclic atelectasis and shear stress.
  • AutoPEEP is therapeutic: Gas trapping maintains recruitment; not an unwanted side effect.
  • Fewer breaths, lower power: Reduced total energy transfer to lung tissue (theoretically less VILI).
  • No paralysis needed: Spontaneous breathing is encouraged; patients can be awake and comfortable.
  • Better basal aeration: Diaphragmatic activity recruits dependent lung regions.
  • Haemodynamics may improve: Spontaneous effort aids venous return.
  • Secretion clearance: High expiratory flow during release may help mobilise secretions (supported by animal studies).
5. Disadvantages & Cautions Con
  • Barotrauma risk: Anecdotal increase in pneumothorax/pneumomediastinum, especially in awake patients who cough on high PEEP.
  • Fluid/vasopressor requirement: Sustained high intrathoracic pressure reduces preload — often needs volume or vasopressors.
  • Unfamiliar mode: Requires clinician comfort; troubleshooting by junior staff can be difficult.
  • RV strain: High pressures may worsen RV afterload; caution in severe pulmonary hypertension or right-to-left shunting.
  • Tidal volumes can be large: With Plow = 0, release volumes may violate lung-protective norms.
  • Derecruitment risk: Brief releases may cause alveolar collapse if Tlow is too long.
  • Without spontaneous breathing: APRV degenerates into inverse-ratio ventilation and loses many benefits.
6. Practical Settings & Troubleshooting

Initial setup

  1. Set Phigh to the current plateau pressure (≈ 28 cm H₂O), not above 30.
  2. Set Plow to 0 cm H₂O.
  3. Set Tlow to 0.3–0.5 s; titrate to 75% of peak expiratory flow.
  4. Set Thigh to ~9× Tlow (e.g. 4.5 s for Tlow 0.5 s).

Troubleshooting

  • Hypoxia persists: Increase Plow (raises autoPEEP, reduces derecruitment) rather than increasing Phigh.
  • Hypercapnia: Reduce Thigh to increase respiratory rate (more releases per minute).
  • Over-distension / high plateau: Reduce Phigh in 2 cm H₂O steps.
  • Haemodynamic instability: Consider fluid bolus or vasopressor; if refractory, reduce Phigh or abandon APRV.
  • As compliance improves: Lengthen Tlow (0.5 s or more) to avoid excessive autoPEEP.
7. Weaning — “Drop and Stretch”

The weaning process gradually converts APRV into CPAP:

  • Drop Phigh by 2 cm H₂O at each step.
  • Stretch Thigh by 0.5–2 seconds at each step.
  • Leave Plow and Tlow essentially unchanged.
  • The patient breathes spontaneously throughout, becoming increasingly comfortable on what is effectively CPAP with intermittent releases.
  • Once Phigh is ~15–20 cm H₂O and Thigh is long, the release breaths become haemodynamically irrelevant — the patient can be transitioned to conventional ventilation or extubated.

Note: Weaning protocols are based on expert opinion, not strong trial evidence.

8. 10 High-Yield Case Examples
1. Post-laparotomy ARDS
Scenario: 58M, emergency laparotomy for perforated viscus, now P/F 110 on PEEP 14.
APRV move: Switch to Phigh 28, Plow 0, Tlow 0.4 s, Thigh 4.0 s. Allow spontaneous breathing once sedation lightened.
Watch: Fluid balance — may need vasopressors due to reduced preload.
2. COVID-19 ARDS with refractory hypoxaemia
Scenario: 45F, P/F 80 on lung-protective ventilation, failed proning.
APRV move: Start Phigh 30, Plow 5, Tlow 0.3 s (stiff lungs), Thigh 3.5 s. Increase Plow to 8 if hypoxia persists.
Watch: Barotrauma — high risk with coughing on high PEEP.
3. Trauma with pulmonary contusions
Scenario: 32M, RTA, bilateral contusions, P/F 140, plateau 32 on PCV.
APRV move: Set Phigh 32 (not higher), Plow 0, Tlow 0.5 s, Thigh 4.5 s.
Watch: Pneumothorax — ensure chest drains are functioning; low threshold for imaging.
4. Severe CAP with ARDS
Scenario: 70F, bilateral consolidation, P/F 95, on noradrenaline 0.15 µg/kg/min.
APRV move: Consider APRV after optimising conventional ventilation. Start Phigh 28, Plow 5, Tlow 0.4 s, Thigh 4.0 s.
Watch: RV function — echo if available; avoid if severe PH.
5. Post-cardiac surgery ARDS
Scenario: 64M, CABG, now P/F 120, moderate RV dysfunction on echo.
APRV move: Relative caution. If used, keep Phigh ≤ 26, Plow 5–8 to avoid RV strain. Monitor CVP and cardiac output.
Watch: RV afterload — APRV may worsen RV failure.
6. ARDS with bronchopleural fistula
Scenario: 50M, necrotising pneumonia, persistent air leak on chest drain.
APRV move: APRV may reduce mean airway pressure compared to conventional modes if Phigh kept low; however, high PEEP can worsen leak.
Watch: Air leak — consider lowering Phigh and Thigh; may not be suitable.
7. Obese patient with ARDS
Scenario: 42F, BMI 48, P/F 130, difficult to oxygenate on conventional ventilation.
APRV move: Phigh may need to be higher (up to 35) due to chest wall compliance. Start Phigh 30, Plow 5, Tlow 0.4 s, Thigh 4.0 s.
Watch: Oesophageal pressure monitoring if available to guide Phigh.
8. Young patient with ARDS from aspiration
Scenario: 28M, aspiration after overdose, P/F 105, compliance improving.
APRV move: Start Phigh 26, Plow 0, Tlow 0.5 s, Thigh 4.5 s. As compliance improves, lengthen Tlow to 0.6 s.
Watch: AutoPEEP — if excessive, Tlow may need lengthening.
9. ARDS with severe metabolic acidosis
Scenario: 55F, DKA + ARDS, pH 7.15, CO₂ 60 on conventional ventilation.
APRV move: APRV may struggle to clear CO₂. Reduce Thigh to 3.5 s (rate ~17/min) to increase minute volume. Consider Plow 0 for maximal expiratory flow.
Watch: CO₂ clearance — may need to abandon APRV if hypercapnia worsens.
10. Weaning failure on conventional ventilation
Scenario: 60M, ARDS recovering, but failing SBTs due to anxiety and high work of breathing.
APRV move: Transition to APRV with Phigh 20, Plow 5, Tlow 0.5 s, Thigh 6 s. Allow spontaneous breathing; gradually “drop and stretch” to CPAP.
Watch: Patient comfort — APRV often reduces sedation needs and improves weaning success.
