▸2 · Why it matters — peri-operative riskAHA 2023 · Price 2021
  • PH is associated with 4× higher peri-operative major adverse cardiovascular events (death, MI, stroke) vs no PH — 8.3% vs 2.0% in 17 million US non-cardiac surgical hospitalisations. AHA 2023
  • Adjusted odds ratio 1.43 (95% CI 1.40–1.46) for the composite of death/MI/stroke; ~2× risk of cardiogenic shock and cardiac arrest.
  • Complications and death usually relate to acute right ventricular failure, typically within the first 48–72 hours.

Procedure-related risk factors

High-risk features
  • Emergency surgery — 15–50% mortality
  • Major / intermediate-risk surgery
  • Duration >2–3 hours
  • Laparoscopy (CO₂ pneumoperitoneum)
  • One-lung ventilation
  • Steep Trendelenburg or prone positioning
  • Orthopaedic cementing / tourniquet release
  • Large anticipated fluid shifts or bleeding
Lower-risk alternatives to consider
  • Non-surgical or palliative options first
  • Local / regional / peripheral nerve block ± minimal sedation
  • Open rather than laparoscopic approach where benefit is marginal
  • Staged or shorter procedures
  • Perform in a specialist PH centre with ICU/HDU backup
▸3 · Physiology & the RV failure spiralAHA 2023 · ATOTW

RV output depends on preload, afterload, contractility and heart rate — each must be optimised. Raised PVR places a pressure load on a right ventricle that is poorly designed to tolerate it. ATOTW 228

  • PAH-targeted therapies
  • Diuretics — reduce wall stress
  • Supplemental oxygen
  • Restore / maintain AV synchrony
  • Correct iron deficiency
  • Inotropes to restore coupling

Why the right coronary circulation is the vulnerable point

  • RV perfusion depends on the aortic root–RV pressure gradient, which in turn depends on cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance.
  • SVR must be aggressively defended — RV ischaemia can trigger a downward spiral into cardiovascular collapse. ATOTW 228
  • RV hypertrophy lengthens capillary-to-myocyte diffusion distance and limits oxygen supply — supply/demand mismatch develops early.
Factors that raise PVR — avoid Hypoxia · hypercarbia · acidaemia · hypothermia · pain · nitrous oxide · high PEEP and lung hyperinflation · protamine · serotonin · thromboxane A₂ · prostaglandin F₂α / PGE₂ · anxiety and stress. ATOTW 228
Factors that lower PVR — may help Mild hypocarbia (hyperventilation) · inhaled nitric oxide · prostacyclin (PGI₂) · nitroglycerine · sodium nitroprusside · isoprenaline · aminophylline · morphine · tolazoline. (Classic ATOTW list; modern practice favours selective pulmonary vasodilators — iNO, inhaled prostacyclin/iloprost, PDE5 inhibitors.) ATOTW 228
▸4 · Pre-operative assessment & optimisationAHA 2023 · Price 2021

→
2 · Risk assessPatient + surgical factors guide decision-making and consent
→
3 · OptimiseTargeted therapy, preload, comorbidities, prehabilitation
→
4 · Intra-operativeAvoid RV dysfunction; maintain cardiac output
→
5 · Post-operativeICU/HDU 48–72 h; continue therapy; early extubation

Timing & work-up (2–4 weeks before surgery is reasonable)

  • Symptoms/signs of decompensation — progressive dyspnoea, syncope, raised JVP, peripheral oedema — predict morbidity and death.
  • Repeat TTE, ECG, creatinine, BNP/NT-proBNP, 6-minute walk test; consider CPET and right heart catheterisation.
  • Echo high-risk features: RV dilatation/dysfunction, TR jet velocity >2.8 m/s, flattened interventricular septum (parasternal short axis), RA enlargement, pericardial effusion.
  • ABG and pulse oximetry (hypoxia/acidosis worsen PVR); PFTs if lung disease; DLCO <32% predicted suggests advanced disease.
  • Treat supraventricular tachycardia — an independent risk factor for death in PH.

TTE can under- or over-estimate PAP in up to half of at-risk patients; right heart catheterisation is required to confirm the diagnosis and for vasodilator challenge. ATOTW 228

Optimisation checklist

  • Continue all PAH-targeted therapy up to and including the day of surgery — give oral drugs with a sip of water even with an NPO order.
  • Newly started targeted therapy: allow 6–12 weeks for maximal effect before elective surgery.
  • Escalate or switch to IV / inhaled where needed — sildenafil IV, prostacyclin IV or inhaled.
  • Never stop prostanoids — rebound PH crisis can be fatal.
  • Continue diuretics pre-op to avoid acute decompensated heart failure.
  • CTEPH: consider pulmonary endarterectomy or balloon pulmonary angioplasty before time-sensitive surgery (e.g. cancer resection).
  • Correct anaemia and iron deficiency; optimise haemoglobin.
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation / prehabilitation improves 6MWD, quality of life and possibly survival.
  • Plan anticoagulation carefully (CTEPH, arrhythmia, prior stroke) — bridge as needed.
  • Book ICU/HDU bed for 48–72 h in advance.

Where should the surgery happen?

Recommendation Planned intermediate- or high-risk procedures should be performed in a centre with PH specialist, peri-operative anaesthetic and post-operative intensive care expertise. If urgent surgery cannot be transferred, seek urgent telephone advice from a PH centre. Most reported good outcomes in well-characterised PAH/CTEPH come from specialist centres. Price 2021 · AHA 2023
Consent Counsel about the risk of peri-operative complications including death, the possible need for mechanical circulatory support (ECMO), and agreed ceilings of care. Give time for a follow-up consultation where possible. Price 2021
▸5 · Anaesthetic drugs — what to use and what to avoidATOTW · AHA · Price
Key principle The technique chosen matters less than how it is executed. There are no comparative trials of induction agents in PH — the priorities are avoiding tachycardia, hypotension, hypoxaemia and hypercarbia. ATOTW 228 · AHA 2023

Adjuncts to blunt laryngoscopy

  • Fentanyl 1–2 µg/kg and lidocaine 50–100 mg IV before laryngoscopy. AHA 2023
  • Moderate-to-high dose opioid (fentanyl 2–7 µg/kg) as part of a balanced induction is most reliable at limiting tachycardia and SVR fall, though recovery is prolonged. Price 2021
  • Rapid sequence induction with careful, brief mask ventilation — avoid high intrathoracic pressures (they acutely reduce preload and cause severe hypotension).
  • Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg or suxamethonium 1–1.5 mg/kg for rapid airway control. Non-depolarising and depolarising relaxants are both safe — choose by airway considerations.

Maintenance

  • Volatile agent + opioid is a standard balanced technique. All modern volatiles have been used safely; no evidence favours one over another.
  • Avoid N₂O — nitrous oxide raises PVR. ATOTW 228 · AHA 2023
  • TIVA alternative: propofol infusion ± opioid.

▸7 · Intra-operative managementAHA 2023 · Price 2021

Haemodynamic goals

  • MAP ≥60 mmHg (many PH centres target higher to protect RV coronary perfusion). AHA 2023
  • Maintain normal sinus rhythm — continue outpatient β-blockers and calcium channel blockers, but do not start them intra-operatively (negative inotropy). Cardiovert new haemodynamically significant arrhythmias promptly.
  • Avoid or mitigate hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis, hypothermia and pain.
  • Maintain baseline RV loading — hypovolaemia ↓MAP and RV perfusion; hypervolaemia overloads the RV. The hypertrophied RV has a narrow optimal preload window and tolerates volume overload poorly.
  • Optimise CVP / preload and RV function with diuretics and PAH-targeted therapy; use inhaled or parenteral pulmonary vasodilators to acutely reduce RV afterload.
  • Positioning is a rapid preload tool: Trendelenburg / leg lift ↑preload; reverse Trendelenburg ↓preload.

Regional anaesthesia

  • Neuraxial anaesthesia can be used safely, but be vigilant about the cardiovascular consequences of sympathetic blockade. There are no α₁ receptors in the pulmonary circulation, so neuraxial block is unlikely to directly affect PVR. ATOTW 228
  • Systemic vasodilation reduces aortic/coronary perfusion pressure and venous return to the right heart.
  • ↓Right atrial filling → ↓atrial receptor stretch → reflex bradycardia. Blockade of cardio-accelerator fibres at T1–T4 causes bradycardia and loss of inotropy.
  • Avoid single-shot spinal — can cause profound hypotension and bradycardia. Prefer titrated epidural or low-dose CSE, with vasopressors immediately available.
  • Peripheral nerve blocks with minimal or no sedation are preferred where feasible.

Specific surgical considerations

  • Laparoscopy: CO₂ insufflation to 12–15 mmHg reduces venous return and CO; diaphragmatic displacement and Trendelenburg worsen lung compliance; rapid insufflation can cause vagal bradycardia → RV distension, impaired right coronary flow, arrest. Use minimal, slow insufflation; increase minute ventilation to clear CO₂; be ready to convert to open. Price 2021
  • Orthopaedic: cement implantation and tourniquet release cause embolic events and abrupt ↑PVR. Price 2021
  • One-lung ventilation: hypoxia, hypercarbia and high airway pressures all raise PVR.
▸8 · Pulmonary hypertensive crisis — recognition & managementPrice 2021 · Condliffe
Recognise early ↑PVR + ↑RV filling pressures + ↓cardiac output + hypotension = a spiral toward cardiovascular collapse. Precipitants include induction of anaesthesia (vasodilation), laryngoscopy, hypoxaemia, positive pressure ventilation, extubation, peri-operative inflammation, micro-pulmonary embolism, arrhythmias and post-operative fluid shifts.

Stepwise management

1 · Treat the precipitantHypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis, pain, arrhythmia, sepsis, anaemia, PE, pneumothorax, drug error
→
2 · 100% O₂ & hyperventilateTarget PaCO₂ 30–35 mmHg; correct acidosis
→
3 · VasopressorsNorepinephrine up to 0.5 µg/kg/min; add low-dose vasopressin (≤0.04 U/min)
→
4 · Reduce PVRInhaled NO, nebulised prostacyclin/iloprost, IV prostacyclin or sildenafil
→
5 · Optimise RVPreload (CVP 8–12 mmHg), inotrope/inodilator (dobutamine, milrinone) + vasopressor
→
6 · EscalateCPR, VA-ECMO / RV assist as a bridge to recovery or transplant
Do not
  • Do not stop pulmonary vasodilators during hypotension.
  • Do not give a large fluid bolus blindly — the hypertrophied RV tolerates volume overload poorly.
  • Do not give a milrinone bolus in a hypovolaemic or hypotensive patient.

Cardiopulmonary resuscitation & ceilings of care

  • Attempts at CPR in PAH have limited success — discuss prognosis and ceilings of care before surgery. Price 2021
  • VA-ECMO may support the RV as a bridge to recovery or transplantation; some centres have percutaneous RV assist devices.
  • Because mortality is very high in patients on extracorporeal support who do not proceed to transplantation, discuss high-risk patients with the local transplant team before elective surgery.
  • In very high-risk cases, consider elective insertion of ECMO vascular sheaths at induction.
▸9 · Post-operative & critical care managementAHA 2023 · Condliffe

Post-operative care

  • Most morbidity and mortality relates to RV dysfunction and occurs up to 48–72 h after apparently uncomplicated surgery — plan ICU/HDU monitoring for at least this period in significant PAH and after major surgery.
  • Continue PAH therapies without interruption. Use in-line filters for patients with right-to-left shunting (Eisenmenger, PFO).
  • Restart anticoagulation as soon as surgically safe, with bridging heparin where indicated.
  • Good analgesia to blunt sympathetic drive and tachycardia — but avoid excessive sedation causing hypercarbia.
  • Early extubation is desirable: spontaneous ventilation reduces intrathoracic pressure and RV stress.
  • Physiotherapy, DVT prophylaxis and early mobilisation.

Critical care — the four-step approach

1 · Treat precipitantsArrhythmia (restore sinus rhythm), sepsis, anaemia, thromboembolism, pericardial effusion, metabolic disturbance, exacerbation of chronic disease
→
2 · Optimise RV preloadLoop diuretics ± aldosterone antagonist ± thiazide; continuous infusion; CVVH if resistant
→
3 · Reduce RV afterloadIV prostanoid (start low, titrate slowly), add PDE5 inhibitor, inhaled NO / nebulised prostanoid
→
4 · Maintain perfusionInotropes ± vasopressors; consider ECMO / transplant referral

Monitoring & targets in ICU

  • Continuous arterial line; central venous access; consider LiDCO/PiCCO or PAC in selected patients.
  • Suggested triggered alerts: cardiac index <2.2 L/min/m², SvO₂ <55%, urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h for 2 consecutive hours.
  • Serial liver and renal function — venous congestion and low-output states cause multi-organ dysfunction.
  • Generally aim for a net negative fluid balance as a starting point in PH-induced RV failure.
  • Echocardiography at the bedside: RV:LV ratio, septal shift, TR severity, pericardial effusion, RV–PA coupling.

Respiratory support

Ventilation strategy matters In a national cohort of >21,000 patients with PH, invasive mechanical ventilation was associated with much higher mortality than non-invasive support (39.1% vs 12.6%, p<0.001) and longer hospital stay (12 vs 7 days). Prefer HFNC/NIV where possible; if intubation is required, do it early and with vasopressors/inotropes ready. AHA 2023

Outcomes in critical care

  • 43 patients with severe heart failure secondary to PAH requiring catecholamines: in-hospital mortality 43%; worse with low admission BP, low sodium, high creatinine, high NT-proBNP and high CRP.
  • 119 patients admitted with right heart failure secondary to PAH: 90-day mortality 29%; respiratory rate, renal function, hyponatraemia and severity of tricuspid regurgitation predicted poor outcome. Condliffe & Kiely
▸10 · Special situationsAHA 2023 · Price 2021 · Condliffe
Obstetric anaesthesia
  • Pregnancy is generally discouraged in PH — maternal mortality historically 30–56%, currently 10–20% in modern series.
  • ERAs and riociguat are contraindicated (category X); PDE5 inhibitors and prostacyclin pathway agonists are considered safe. Parenteral prostacyclin is preferred because it is easily titrated.
  • Deliver electively at 34–36 weeks by planned caesarean section under combined spinal–epidural (minimises SVR swings) in a specialist centre.
  • Avoid bolus oxytocin — it increases PVR and reduces SVR; use a low-dose infusion. Avoid nitrous oxide.
  • The immediate post-partum period is the highest-risk time (fluid shifts, autotransfusion) — monitor in critical care for at least 72 hours with arterial and central lines.
  • Multidisciplinary team: experienced obstetrician, PH specialist and anaesthetist.

AHA 2023 notes no prospective comparisons of epidural vs spinal vs CSE vs general anaesthesia — all have been described. Low-dose neuraxial with vasopressors titrated to maintain MAP at or above baseline, with inotropes and pulmonary vasodilators immediately available, is advised.

Laparoscopic surgery
  • CO₂ pneumoperitoneum at 12–15 mmHg reduces venous return and cardiac output; diaphragmatic displacement and Trendelenburg worsen lung compliance and FRC.
  • Rapid CO₂ insufflation can cause vagal bradycardia → RV distension, impaired right coronary flow and arrest.
  • Within 5 minutes of insufflation, rising end-tidal CO₂ may require increased minute ventilation or deflation to avoid a rise in PVR.
  • Mitigations: minimal and slow insufflation, low insufflation pressure, early conversion to open surgery if haemodynamics deteriorate.
  • No studies compare laparoscopic vs open surgery in PH; the general benefits of laparoscopy (pain, bleeding) still apply when planning.
Orthopaedic surgery
  • Lower limb joint surgery carries a risk of intra-operative pulmonary embolism of bone marrow, cement and debris — increasing PVR and precipitating acute RV failure.
  • Cement implantation syndrome and tourniquet release are the classic triggers.
  • Coding studies show a four-fold adjusted risk of mortality (2.4% vs 0.6%) for any PH undergoing total hip replacement — higher still in the PAH group (5%).
Liver transplantation / portopulmonary hypertension
  • Up to 5% of patients on the liver transplant waiting list develop portopulmonary hypertension.
  • Patients with mPAP >35 mmHg are generally deemed unsuitable for orthotopic liver transplantation unless they respond to PAH therapy.
  • Mortality data: mPAP >50 mmHg — 100% mortality; 35–50 mmHg — 35%; <35 mmHg — no deaths.
  • Consider PVR and RV function alongside mPAP, since high-output liver disease can raise mPAP with normal PVR.
Sedation & endoscopy
  • Even mild sedation can cause hypoxaemia, hypercapnia and increased PVR in high-risk PH patients — sedation is not risk-free.
  • Bronchoscopic biopsy, TBNA and airway manipulation can trigger arrhythmias and cardiovascular complications; oxygen use reduces complication rates.
  • Gastrointestinal endoscopy: vagal stimulation may precipitate cardiac arrest in severe PH. In one French reference-centre series of 37 procedures under sedation/GA, major complications to 28 days occurred in 14% (severe hypotension, arrhythmias, RV failure) — no deaths, but this exceeded the 1.8% cardiopulmonary adverse event rate seen in ASA 3 patients undergoing GI procedures.
  • Patients with severe PH requiring endoscopy should be monitored by experienced staff in PH centres.
Congenital heart disease & Eisenmenger syndrome
  • Eisenmenger syndrome: peri-operative mortality ~7% in an older cohort; more recent data show systemic hypotension in 26%, oxygen desaturation in 17% and 30-day mortality 3.8%.
  • Single-ventricle / Fontan physiology depends on passive venous return — maintain preload, avoid high PEEP and high intrathoracic pressure.
  • With shunts, prevent increased right-to-left shunting (which can spiral into hypoxaemia, acidosis and hypotension) by keeping the PVR:SVR ratio low.
  • Neuraxial blockade can enhance right-to-left shunting in Eisenmenger syndrome or PAH with a PFO.
  • Use in-line filters for all IV lines in patients with right-to-left shunts.
Emergency surgery
  • Highest risk of all — 30-day mortality 15–50% in non-cardiac, non-obstetric surgery.
  • Same principles as critical care: optimise preload, reduce RV afterload, maintain RV coronary perfusion.
  • Use the most pulmonary-selective vasodilators (iNO, inhaled prostanoids) to acutely reduce PVR without dropping SVR.
  • Normalise haemoglobin, arterial pH, oxygenation and lung volumes on positive pressure to avoid acute PVR rises.
  • Aggressive analgesia — untreated pain acutely raises pulmonary vascular tone.
  • Preserving a low PVR:SVR ratio is the key objective; this may mandate higher-than-normal MAP goals during a crisis.

Diagnostic pitfalls

  • Symptoms are non-specific (exertional dyspnoea) — diagnosis is often delayed.
  • TTE under- or over-estimates PAP in up to half of at-risk patients; right heart catheterisation is required for confirmation. ATOTW 228
  • An enlarged LA, LV hypertrophy and elevated E/e' suggest left heart disease as the cause.
  • Isotope perfusion scanning is the most sensitive way to exclude CTEPH; CTPA also assesses lungs and heart.
  • An acute vasodilator challenge (usually inhaled NO) identifies the ~10% with a predominantly vasoconstrictive phenotype who may respond to high-dose calcium channel blockers.

Therapeutic pearls

  • Treat the underlying cause of deterioration first — arrhythmia and sepsis are common and reversible.
  • Atrial flutter occurs in up to 25% of pre-capillary PH patients; restoring sinus rhythm improves outcomes more than rate control alone.
  • An unexplained rising CRP in a patient on IV prostanoids should prompt a search for catheter-related bloodstream infection — often with no external signs.
  • Continuous veno-venous haemofiltration may be considered for resistant RV failure / volume overload.
  • Balloon atrial septostomy is a potential therapy for severe PAH but is rarely performed; high RA pressure and significant hypoxaemia are risk factors for mortality.

Do not…

  • Do not interrupt PAH-targeted therapy, especially prostanoids
  • Do not give nitrous oxide
  • Do not allow hypoxaemia, hypercarbia or acidosis
  • Do not use high PEEP or high plateau pressures
  • Do not allow marked falls in venous return or SVR
  • Do not use drugs that cause myocardial depression without vasopressor cover
  • Do not allow tachycardia or bradycardia to persist
  • Do not perform a single-shot spinal in severe PH
  • Do not flush a prostacyclin infusion line
  • Do not give nitrates with a PDE5 inhibitor
  • Do not give a milrinone bolus to a hypovolaemic patient

Always…

  • Apply defibrillator pads before induction
  • Site an arterial line before induction where possible
  • Have vasopressors drawn up and running at induction
  • Keep the patient warm, pain-free and euvolaemic
  • Book ICU/HDU for 48–72 hours
  • Involve the PH team and, if relevant, the ECMO/transplant team early
  • Discuss ceilings of care and resuscitation status pre-operatively
