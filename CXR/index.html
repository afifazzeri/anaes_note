How to use this template: Present every film in the same order, every time. Structure earns marks even when the film is normal — a calm, organised presentation of a normal film scores higher than a disorganised hunt for pathology. Say the 5-line summary first, then expand with A–H only if asked or if you find something.

Part 1 — The 5-Line Summary (say this first, ~30 seconds)

1. Film type: "This is a [PA / AP] [erect / supine] chest radiograph of [patient name, age, sex], taken on [date]."
2. Quality: "The film is of adequate quality — adequate penetration, no significant rotation, and adequate inspiration. The framing includes the lower neck down to the upper abdomen."
3. Hardware: "There are no lines or tubes in situ." or "There is an ETT with the tip [3–5 cm above the carina], a CVC with the tip at the [cavoatrial junction], and an NGT passing below the diaphragm into the stomach."
4. Systematic findings: "The airway is central. The lungs are clear and symmetrical. The heart size and mediastinal contours are normal. Both hemidiaphragms are smooth, the right normally higher than the left, with sharp costophrenic angles. The bones and soft tissues are unremarkable."
5. Impression: "In summary, this is a normal chest radiograph. I would correlate clinically and compare with any previous films."

Part 2 — The A–H Systematic Run-Through

Use this order every time. State each step out loud, even if normal.

D. Diaphragm "Both hemidiaphragms are smooth and well defined; the right is normally 1.5–2 cm higher than the left. There is no free air under the diaphragm." Flattening (hyperinflation), elevation (phrenic palsy, collapse, subphrenic collection), pneumoperitoneum
E. Effusion & pleura "Both costophrenic angles are sharp. There is no pleural effusion and no pneumothorax." Blunted angles, meniscus, loculation, visible visceral pleural edge, absent peripheral markings
F. Fields (lungs) "The lung fields are clear and symmetrical, with no consolidation, nodule or mass." Compare zone-by-zone side to side; review apices, behind clavicles, retrocardiac space, behind hila
G. Gastric bubble & upper abdomen "The gastric bubble is normal. There is no free air or dilated bowel loop." Gastric bubble position, free air, bowel gas pattern, calcifications
H. Hidden review areas "Finally, I have reviewed the apices, the retrocardiac space, the subdiaphragmatic region and the soft tissues." Most commonly missed pathology: Pancoast tumour, retrocardiac consolidation, free air, subtle hilar mass

Part 3 — Fill-In Checklist (tick as you go)

Before you speak
  • Patient identifiers on the film match the request (name, DOB, MRN, date, time)
  • Previous film available for comparison
  • Projection (PA / AP) and position (erect / supine) noted
Quality (RIPE)
  • Rotation — medial ends of clavicles equidistant from the spinous process
  • Inspiration — 9–10 posterior ribs (or 5–6 anterior ribs) above the diaphragm
  • Penetration — thoracic vertebrae visible behind the cardiac shadow
  • Exposure/framing — lower neck to upper abdomen fully included
Hardware
  • ETT tip 3–5 cm above the carina
  • CVC tip at the cavoatrial junction
  • NGT tip below the diaphragm, in the stomach
  • No misplaced, kinked or fractured device; no new pneumothorax
A–H systematic
  • A — Trachea central, carina at T4–T5, main bronchi patent
  • B — Ribs, clavicles, scapulae and spine intact; no soft tissue gas or mass
  • C — CTR < 50%; mediastinum not widened; borders preserved; hila normal
  • D — Hemidiaphragms smooth; right slightly higher; no free air
  • E — Costophrenic angles sharp; no pleural edge or effusion
  • F — Lung fields clear and symmetrical; no consolidation, nodule or mass
  • G — Gastric bubble normal; no free air or dilated bowel
  • H — Hidden areas reviewed: apices, retrocardiac, subdiaphragmatic, soft tissues
Closing
  • Positive and important negative findings stated explicitly
  • Single most likely diagnosis given
  • Next step stated (compare with prior, CT, clinical correlation)

Part 5 — How to Close (Conclusion Formula)

Findings → Diagnosis → Next step.
• Findings: one sentence summarising the positive findings and the important negatives.
• Diagnosis: the single most likely diagnosis, plus one alternative if genuinely uncertain.
• Next step: "I would compare with previous films / correlate clinically / proceed to CT / repeat film after line repositioning."

Part 6 — Common Exam Traps

  • Jumping straight to pathology without stating technical adequacy.
  • Forgetting to compare left with right and to look at the apices and retrocardiac space.
  • Calling AP magnification cardiomegaly — always qualify heart size by projection.
  • Missing a subtle apical pneumothorax — trace the lung edge deliberately.
  • Ignoring lines and tubes on an ICU film — these are often the reason for the film.
  • Giving a long differential without ever committing to one most likely diagnosis.
  • Not mentioning free air under the diaphragm — a classic missed finding.

Before interpreting pathology, systematically verify image quality and technical adequacy to avoid diagnostic errors.

Device / LineTarget Anatomical LocationRadiological Landmark & Notes
Endotracheal Tube (ETT) Mid-trachea, ~3–5 cm above the Carina (level of T4–T5). Head flexion/extension moves tip up to 2 cm. Too deep → right main bronchus intubation and left lung atelectasis.
Central Venous Line (CVL / CVP) Cavoatrial Junction / Recess. Junction between the Superior Vena Cava (SVC) and the upper right atrium. Ensures accurate CVP monitoring and prevents vascular erosion.
Nasogastric Tube (NGT) Body/fundus of stomach, well below the diaphragm. Must follow midline down esophagus, cross the gastroesophageal junction, and tip must rest in the stomach lumen. Verify before feed administration!

Mediastinal Width & Measurement

Measurement Rule: Measure mediastinal width at the level of the aortic knob.
• Widened Mediastinum: Measured width > 6 cm on upright PA film or > 8 cm on supine AP film.

Mediastinal Lines & Stripes (Radiopaedia Detail)

A line is soft tissue bounded by aerated lung on both sides; a stripe is broader soft tissue opacity.

Line / StripeAnatomical Origin & LocationClinical Relevance
Posterior Junctional Line Four pleural layers abutt behind trachea and esophagus. Visible superiorly; displacement indicates posterior mediastinal mass.
Anterior Junctional Line Pleural abutment behind manubrium, anterior to heart. Oblique line over upper sternum.
Azygoesophageal Recess / Line Interface where right lung enters recess behind esophagus & azygous vein. Loss of recess concavity indicates subcarinal/posterior mass or adenopathy.
Paratracheal Stripes (Right & Left) Soft tissue adjacent to trachea (right stripe < 4 mm thick). Widening suggests paratracheal lymphadenopathy, Pancoast tumor, or hemorrhage.
Paravertebral Lines Pleural reflection along lateral margins of vertebral bodies. Displacement suggests paravertebral hematoma, fracture, or neurogenic tumor.

Normal Heart Position & Size

  • Thoracic Position: Normally 1/3 of cardiac diameter lies to the right and 2/3 to the left of the thoracic vertebrae spinous processes.
  • Borders: Right atrium forms the right border; Left ventricle forms the left border. (Note: Right ventricle is an anterior structure on axial/CT projections).
  • Cardiomegaly: Cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) > 50% on upright PA film.

Tracheobronchial Tree Anatomy

  • Trachea: Central airway, bifurcates at the Carina (T4–T5).
  • Right Main Bronchus: Wider, shorter, and more vertically inclined. Clinical consequence: Aspiration and foreign bodies preferentially lodge in the right side.
  • Left Main Bronchus: Longer, narrower, and more horizontal/oblique.
  • Right Divisions: Right Upper Lobe (RUL) bronchus + Bronchus Intermedius (which divides into RML and RLL bronchi).
  • Left Divisions: Left Upper Lobe (LUL) bronchus + Left Lower Lobe (LLL) bronchus.

Hilar Anatomy (Radiopaedia Comprehensive Breakdown)

HilumPulmonary Artery Path & BranchesVenous & Bronchial StructuresHilar Angle / Morphology
Right Hilum Right Pulmonary Artery passes anterior to right main bronchus & posterior to SVC. Divides into:
• Truncus Anterior: Medial to RUL bronchus.
• Right Interlobar Artery: Dense structure ("little finger") lateral to bronchus intermedius.		 Right Superior Pulmonary Vein forms the superior limb. RUL bronchus seen end-on/medially. Hilar Angle: Sharp V-shaped concavity formed between the interlobar artery and superior pulmonary vein.
Left Hilum Left Pulmonary Artery arches over and behind the left main bronchus. Gives off Left Interlobar Artery (inferior limb) and superior lobe branches. Left Superior Pulmonary Vein drains posteriorly to left atrium. Left main bronchus lies inferior to artery (Hyparterial bronchus). Left hilum is normally 1–2 cm higher than the right hilum due to the left pulmonary artery arching over the bronchus.

Lobes & Fissures Geography

  • High Apex of Lower Lobes: Posteriorly, both lower lobes extend high up to the level of T4 (left) and T5 (right). Upper zone opacities on frontal CXR can be in lower lobes!
  • Horizontal Fissure (Right Lung): Runs horizontally from hilum to anterior 4th–6th rib, separating RUL from RML.
  • Oblique Fissures (Bilateral): Extend from T4/T5 posteriorly down to diaphragm 3–4 cm behind sternum.
  • Luftsichel Sign: Hyper-expansion of the LLL superior segment around the aortic arch in LUL collapse.

Parenchymal Opacities (Prof. Rowi Classification)

Pattern TypeRadiological AppearanceDifferential Causes
Reticular Opacity Net-like linear opacities. Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis, pulmonary edema.
Air Space / Homogeneous Opacity Uniform fluffy consolidation with air bronchograms. Bacterial pneumonia, alveolar hemorrhage, ARDS.
Heterogeneous Opacity Patchy, non-uniform consolidation. Bronchopneumonia, aspiration, multifocal infection.
Nodular Opacity Discrete round opacities (<3 cm). Metastases, granulomas (TB, fungal), rheumatoid nodules.
Mixed Reticulonodular Combination of lines and small nodules. Miliary TB, sarcoidosis, pneumoconiosis.

Pleural Space & Effusions

  • Costophrenic Angles: Sharp acute angles. Blunting indicates pleural effusion (>200 mL on PA film; posterior costophrenic angle on lateral film is most sensitive, detecting >50 mL).
  • Pneumothorax: Visible visceral pleural edge with absent lung markings peripherally.
  • Loculated Effusion vs. Empyema: D-shaped or lenticular opacities along chest wall/fissures.

Diaphragm Curvature & Levels

  • Normal Position: Right hemidiaphragm is ~1.5–2 cm higher than the left due to underlying liver.
  • Flattened Diaphragm: Sign of severe hyperinflation (COPD/asthma).
  • 6 Causes of Abnormal Diaphragm Elevation: Phrenic nerve palsy, lower lobe atelectasis/collapse, subphrenic abscess, hepatomegaly/splenomegaly, eventration, volume loss.
  • Free Air (Pneumoperitoneum): Crescentic lucency under hemidiaphragms (requires upright positioning).

Lateral View Hemidiaphragm Differentiation

Left vs. Right Hemidiaphragm on Lateral CXR:
• Left Hemidiaphragm: Abuts the cardiac shadow anteriorly → loses its anterior border contour.
• Right Hemidiaphragm: Clear unbroken contour extending all the way to the anterior chest wall.

Critical Review Areas (Where Pathology is Commonly Missed!)

  1. Apices: Behind clavicles (Pancoast tumors, early TB).
  2. Retrocardiac Space: Behind the heart (left lower lobe consolidation, mass).
  3. Subdiaphragmatic Region: Free air, stomach bubble, subphrenic abscess.
  4. Hilar Regions: Subtle lymphadenopathy or early central mass.
  5. Soft Tissues & Scapulae: Mastectomy, subcutaneous emphysema, chest wall masses.
RegionStructures to EvaluatePathological Findings
Bones & Skeleton Ribs (count 9–10 posterior or 5–6 anterior above diaphragm), Clavicles, Scapulae, Humeral heads, Cervical/Thoracic vertebrae. Rib fractures (flail chest), osteolytic/osteoblastic metastases, osteoporosis, rib notching (coarctation of aorta), pedicle erosion.
Soft Tissues Chest wall thickness, male vs. female breast shadows, neck and axillary regions. Subcutaneous emphysema (surgical asthma), fat stripe vs. air, breast shadow asymmetry (mastectomy), axillary lymphadenopathy.
Upper Abdomen Stomach gas bubble (triangular shape), subdiaphragmatic renal areas, bowel gas pattern. Dilated bowel loops (trauma/ileus), ascites, pancreatic calcification, renal stones, gallbladder stones.
Clinical Condition / PathogenCharacteristic CXR Findings & Features
Staphylococcus aureus Multifocal heterogeneous opacities, rapid cavitation, pneumatoceles, empyema.
Fungal Infections Nodular opacities, upper lobe cavitary lesions, halo sign or air-crescent sign.
Tuberculosis (TB) Apical parenchymal opacities, cavitary lesions, miliary reticulonodular pattern, ghon complex.
Pulmonary Embolism (PE) May be normal film; classic signs include Westermark sign (regional oligemia), Hampton's hump (wedge-shaped opacity), or pleural effusion.
