🐞 Melioidosis Quick Guide for anaesthetists & intensivists

🐜 Bug's Life edition NAG 2026
🩺 Clinical Presentation & Suspicion ▾

Incubation 1–21 days (mean 9 days); latent up to 62 years. Asymptomatic seroconversion common (up to 50% in endemic areas).

Common presentations: Community‑acquired pneumonia (commonest), septicaemia, multiple abscesses (liver, spleen, prostate), septic arthritis, skin/soft tissue infection.

When to suspect acute melioidosis:

  • Residence/work in endemic area (Jengka, Maran, Jerantut, FELDA settlements).
  • Diabetes mellitus (most common predisposing factor).
  • High‑grade fever (acute or prolonged).
  • History of exposure to contaminated dust, soil, or water.
  • Progressive pneumonia not responding to common antibiotics (mortality >50%).
  • Hepatomegaly and/or splenomegaly – suspect multiple abscesses.
⚠️ Warning signs: Acute high‑grade fever, systemic symptoms (nausea, poor appetite, lethargy), cough (productive or non‑productive), difficulty breathing. These indicate severe disease — discuss with physician.

Paediatric notes: Co‑morbidity less common (10–20%). Localised infection (head/neck, parotid) common; septic shock in 38.4% of septicaemic children, mortality 80%.

Source: Pahang Melioidosis Guideline, Sections 5.0–5.3.
🔬 Investigations & Sampling ▾

Radiology: CXR, USG abdomen, CT scan (indicated for cerebral abscess, prostatic abscess).

Laboratory: FBC, UFEME, renal/liver function, blood sugar. Blood cultures ×2 (different sites, before antibiotics). Urine culture, cultures from abscess/joint/CSF/sputum/throat. PCR for blood, body secretions, urine.

Serology (IFAT IgM): Positive ≥1:80 in endemic area. Asymptomatic titre up to 1:160 may not be significant but requires follow‑up. All specimens analysed at IMR.

Adjunctive Trimethoprim/Sulphamethoxazole (TMP/SMX): May add for neurological, osteomyelitis, septic arthritis, genitourinary (including prostatic abscess), and skin/soft tissue infections. Dosing: <40 kg: 160/800 mg PO q12h; 40–60 kg: 240/1200 mg PO q12h; >60 kg: 320/1600 mg PO q12h. Consider de‑escalation to ceftazidime once stable. Graded dosing (for >60 kg): Day 1–2: 40/200 mg q12h; Day 3–4: 80/400 mg q12h; Day 5–6: 160/800 mg q12h; Day 7–8: 240/1200 mg q12h; Day 9 onwards: 320/1600 mg q12h. Full dose immediately for neurological melioidosis or critically ill with multifocal abscess. For <60 kg or renal impairment, consult ID physician. Cessation of IV therapy and start of eradication phase only once full planned TMP/SMX dose is reached.

Duration of intensive therapy: Skin/soft tissue or mild pneumonia: 2 weeks; Bacteraemia with no foci: 2–4 weeks; Complicated pneumonia, prostatic, deep‑seated foci, septic arthritis: 4–6 weeks; Osteomyelitis: 6 weeks; Neurologic/CNS or arterial infection (e.g., mycotic aneurysm): 8 weeks.

🏥 Public Health & Prophylaxis ▾

Notification: Melioidosis is not notifiable under Act 342, but Pahang State Health Department mandates administrative notification using Appendix 7 and registration in the Pahang melioidosis registry.

Case definition: Clinical: fever and/or pneumonia and/or single/multiple abscesses AND predisposing factors (esp. diabetes) AND history of exposure. Confirmed: positive culture or PCR or four‑fold rise in serology.

Post‑exposure prophylaxis: Co‑trimoxazole 2–4 tabs BD for 3 weeks within 24 hours of high‑probability exposure. Amoxicillin/clavulanate can be used if intolerant.

Outbreak definition: >1 confirmed case with epidemiological link within 21 days. Report within 24 hours to district health office / CPRC.

Co‑infection note: Leptospirosis and scrub typhus are common in endemic areas. Add Doxycycline to anti‑melioidosis antibiotics if co‑infection strongly suspected.
Source: Pahang Melioidosis Guideline, Sections 8.0–9.2.
🔄 Follow‑up & Relapse Monitoring ▾

Adult: Monitor clinically and radiologically. Follow‑up at least 5 years. Warn about lifelong risk of relapse. During eradication phase (20 weeks) review every 8 weeks; check diabetic control, weight, appetite, temperature, CXR (if pneumonia), USG/CT (if abscesses), TWBC/ESR/CRP. Prolonged fever ≥5 days → check compliance, blood culture.

Paediatric: Total follow‑up 5 years if asymptomatic. During maintenance: 1–2 monthly at hospital with paediatrician. After completion: 4–6 monthly for 2 years (hospital), then 6–12 monthly for 3 years (nearest Klinik Kesihatan). Monitor for relapse and late complications.

Source: Pahang Melioidosis Guideline, Section 7.4.
